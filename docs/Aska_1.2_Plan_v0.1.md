# Aska 1.2 — Plan: the paper mode

## Version 0.1 — 9 October 2026 (draft for the owner's approval; no code until approved)

- **What this is:** the work list for release 1.2, decided by the project owner on 9 October 2026 after the 1.1.0 release: **the paper mode** (roadmap item RM-01; Decision Record D-02 "paper/OTP mode on the same container" and D-12 "paper materials"; *Option C Concept* §2.7 and use cases 4 and 5; *Research* report 06 on pen-and-paper methods). The owner also decided the order of the work **after** 1.2: mobile clients — GrapheneOS first, then a standard Samsung phone, then iPhone (§"After 1.2").
- **Status:** draft v0.1. Nothing in it is built. The owner approves or amends this plan, then step 1 starts.
- **What the paper mode is for:** the cases where no device can be trusted at the moment of use (Level 3 in the concept paper): a note enciphered with a **one-time pad** that was generated once, on a device with its radios off, printed on water-soluble paper, and is enciphered and deciphered **by hand**; and the **cross-medium** form, where an ordinary Block and its key Shares travel on paper as QR cards instead of through a relay. The one-time pad is the only cipher with a proof of unbreakability, and it is deniable by construction (any ciphertext deciphers to any text of the same length under a suitably chosen pad). History says it fails on operations, not mathematics — pad reuse, deciphering on a computer that remembers, printer tracking dots, impressions on the sheet below, photographs of pads — so most of this release is making those failures hard.
- **Order of work:** design first (a short design change document, as DC-01 and DC-02 were, approved by the owner before code), then the core, then the command-line client, then the graphical client and printing, then the guide with a pen-and-paper trial by the owner, then the release. Each step ends with every gate green and a delivered update; the owner pushes it and the automatic checks run before the next step starts.
- **Compatibility:** nothing in 1.2 changes the Block Format, the wire protocol or the relay. The pad is a new kind of paper artefact, not a new Block type; its ciphertext is a string of digits that can travel over any channel — including, when convenient, as the text of an ordinary Aska note. Block cards and Share cards are the existing Block and Share encodings printed as QR, and a 1.0.x or 1.1 client can read a Share card's text. Profiles are unchanged.

## Design decisions proposed for the owner (settled in step 1's design document)

| # | Question | Proposal | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-1 | What the pad enciphers | **Digits.** Text is converted with a **straddling checkerboard** printed on the worksheet (letters, digits, a few punctuation marks → 1 or 2 digits each), then each digit is added to the pad digit modulo 10; the receiver subtracts. Pages of **400 message characters** (about 480 pad digits with the checkerboard's two-digit codes), as in use case 5. | The checkerboard-plus-mod-10 method is the one with a century of field use and the simplest arithmetic a person can do reliably on a single sheet. Letters-mod-26 is slower by hand and less forgiving. |
| P-2 | Integrity | **Two tiers.** (a) **Device tag, 64 bits:** a Carter–Wegman one-time MAC over a 61-bit prime field (forgery probability about 2⁻⁵⁵ for a page of 500 digits), with its key digits printed on the page; verified by the app from a scan or typed digits. (b) **Hand tag, four digits:** a one-time *linear* universal hash over the prime 9 973 (each message digit multiplied by a 4-digit pad value, summed modulo 9 973, plus a one-time offset), which a person can compute with pencil and a column of small multiplications; forgery probability about 1 in 10 000 per attempt. The page says which tier it carries; the booklet default is both. | A 64-bit tag cannot be computed by hand in reasonable time; a four-digit tag is the strongest a person will actually compute. Stating the two honestly is better than printing a tag nobody verifies. The hand tag's key costs about four pad digits per message digit (~1 900 per 400-character page, or half that if the design hashes two-digit groups), so the page layout (P-5) must budget for it. |
| P-3 | Randomness | **Two independent sources, mixed.** The operating system's CSPRNG and **one physical source contributed at generation time**: by default camera frames through `aska-scan` (sensor noise, 1.1.0's capture layer), or dice rolls typed in, or keyboard timing. Each is conditioned (SHA3-512) and the pad is derived from the XOR of the two conditioned streams; a pad is refused if the physical source delivered too little (entropy estimate shown and a minimum enforced). Statistical self-test (frequency, runs, serial) on every generated booklet before printing. | The research report's first rule: never trust one source. The camera path gives most users a second source without extra hardware; dice keep it possible with no camera at all. |
| P-4 | Printing and the spool problem | **Printing goes through the desktop print dialog** (GTK print operation → CUPS), **but a pad booklet is printed only when the spool cannot persist:** the app checks that the print spool directory is on volatile storage (Tails, or a RAM-backed `/var/spool/cups`) and otherwise refuses to print the pad and offers the **hand-copy template** (large digits on screen, grouped in fives, one page at a time) or a **stateless reader/printer** (RM-07, later). Block cards and Share cards may be printed anywhere: they are noise, and a Share alone is worthless. **Monochrome only** is stated, not enforceable: the app prints a tracking-dot warning on the checklist. Never a PDF or image file on disk (CLI-03 stands): the command-line client writes the page images to standard output for piping to `lp`. | A CUPS spool keeps a copy of every job on disk unless the system is amnesic; printing a pad from an ordinary desktop would put the pad on the disk that the whole design says must hold nothing (research 06 §5; the Cuban cases). |
| P-5 | Page layout | One page = page number, pad digits in groups of five with row and column indices, the tag keys, the checksum of the page (so a mis-copied page is caught), and the printed rules ("use once; write on glass; dissolve after use; never photograph"). Two booklets are printed as **A** and **B** copies with the same page numbers. A **worksheet** page carries the checkerboard, the add/subtract tables and the hand-tag procedure. QR of the page's digits is printed alongside for the device path (P-6). | The page must be usable by a person with no device and no memory of the app. |
| P-6 | Device path | The same pad can be used **on the device**: scan a pad page (camera, 1.1.0) or type its digits, then encipher or decipher a note in locked memory; the page is marked used in the Session only (nothing persists); the app tells the user to destroy the page. This is the path for a user who has a trusted amnesic device but no relay. | Gives the pad to people who will not do 400 additions by hand, with the same paper discipline. |
| P-7 | Cross-medium cards | **Block cards:** a class-1 Block (4 096 bytes) printed as two QR codes (version 40, binary mode) or four smaller ones for easier scanning, with page order marks; class 2 (16 KiB) as six to eight codes; **class 3 is not offered on paper.** Read back with the camera (1.1.0) into a Session as if fetched from a relay. **Share cards:** the existing `askas1…` Share as QR plus its words, one card per Share, for the succession use case (3-of-5 on durable paper). | Uses what 1.1.0 built (in-process scanning) and the existing encodings; no format change. |
| P-8 | Not in 1.2 | Splitting a pad booklet k-of-n between couriers (Hamming-XOR or codex32) — later; the stateless single-board reader/generator (RM-07) — later; any hand cipher other than the one-time pad — never (research 06 §2: none has a proof); flash paper — never recommended (D-12). | Keeps 1.2 to what has a proof and can be tested by one owner with a pen. |

## After 1.2 — mobile clients (owner decision, 9 October 2026)

The owner decided the order of work after the paper mode: **mobile clients, in this order: (1) GrapheneOS, (2) a standard Samsung phone, (3) iPhone.** Recorded here so that the next plan starts from it; nothing is designed yet. What is already decided or reserved for it:

- The Block Format has **KDF profile 2** (Argon2id, 64 MiB) reserved for mobile (S-01), so a phone can open and seal Blocks that desktops read.
- RM-02 in the Decision Record and DC-01 §6a: on a platform without an external Tor, the client must provide its own Tor connection and its bridge configuration from the first release, as the ordinary way its Tor is set up — not as a fallback — and it never fetches bridges itself (DC-01 §9 boundary unchanged). Where a platform Tor exists (Orbot on Android), it is used as on the desktop (option A).
- The first two targets are **one Android client** built from the Rust core (`aska-core` is pure Rust with no C library, so it is expected to build for Android targets; the Linux-only parts — doctor probes, Tor control, memory-locking details — need platform versions; the UI is new): GrapheneOS is the reference platform, where the platform's own protections (per-app sandboxing, auto-reboot, duress features) carry much of the endpoint guidance; the Samsung step is the same application validated and documented on a stock Android phone with Google services, where the guidance has to say plainly what the platform does not give. Distribution for both: a signed APK from the release page, verified like the desktop files, before any store listing is considered.
- The iPhone is a **separate client** sharing the Rust core through a C interface; it will need its own pre-study because iOS gives an application fewer of the controls the desktop design relies on (no equivalent of the desktop's locked, dump-excluded memory guarantees, no way to forbid screen capture — only to detect it — and distribution tied to an Apple developer identity, which touches participation deniability for the publisher, not the user).
- **Plan 1.3 (or 2.0) for mobile** is written after 1.2.0 ships, starting with a mobile pre-study (research report 08: Tor on each platform, memory and capture controls, distribution and identity, what the amnesic rule means on a phone) — the same way this project began.
